

## 1. Introduction to Information Systems

### 1.1 What is an Information System?

An **Information System (IS)** is a combination of **hardware, software, telecommunications networks, people, data and procedures** used to collect, create, process, store and distribute useful information, typically within an organization.

The main objective of an information system is to:

> **Collect data → Process data → Produce information → Communicate information → Support users and decisions**

Information systems define the flow of information within an organization and help businesses perform their operations efficiently and interact with customers.

**Examples:** Online banking, e-commerce websites, railway reservation systems, payroll systems and inventory-management systems.

---

## 2. Components of an Information System

### 2.1 Computer Hardware

**Hardware** refers to the physical equipment used for input, output, processing and storage.

It includes devices such as computers, processors, monitors, keyboards, scanners, printers, storage devices, tablets and other peripheral equipment. The hardware required depends on the type and size of the organization.

**Example:** In a warehouse, barcode scanners and computers are hardware used to record and process inventory.

---

### 2.2 Computer Software

**Software** consists of programs and instructions that control and coordinate hardware and process data.

It can be broadly classified into:

|Type|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**System Software**|Controls and manages computer hardware|Operating system|
|**Application Software**|Performs specific business/user tasks|Inventory-management software|
|**Procedures**|Instructions describing how the system should be used|Steps for processing an order|

**Example:** An inventory-management program that tracks product stock is application software because it performs a specific business function.

---

### 2.3 Databases

A **database** contains organized data that can be stored, accessed and managed efficiently.

Data consists of raw facts such as customer orders, product quantities, addresses and sales records. Database software and **Database Management Systems (DBMS)** help organizations store, organize, retrieve and manage this data.

> **Data → Database → Processing → Information**

A system depends heavily on its data because software needs data to perform meaningful processing.

---

### 2.4 Human Resources

People are essential to an information system because they develop, operate, manage and use it.

**IS personnel** include systems analysts, programmers, computer operators and other IT and managerial personnel.

**End users** include accountants, engineers, salespersons, clerks, managers and customers.

The ultimate purpose of an information system is to provide useful information to its users.

---

### 2.5 Telecommunications Networks

Telecommunications networks enable information and data to move between different computers, devices and users.

They allow organizations to connect employees, customers, branches and systems and support activities such as communication, online transactions and information sharing.

---

## 3. Case Study — FreshCart

**Scenario:** FreshCart, an online grocery startup in Pune, uses computers, scanners and delivery-tracking tablets in its warehouse. It also uses a website, mobile application and inventory-management software. Customer orders, stock levels and delivery addresses are stored centrally.

**Q1. What component do the warehouse computers and delivery tablets represent?**

**Answer:** **Computer Hardware** — they are physical devices used for input, processing and other system activities.

**Q2. What component does the inventory-management program represent?**

**Answer:** **Computer Software**, specifically application software, because it performs the business task of managing inventory.

**Q3. If the central system crashes and order records are lost, which component has failed?**

**Answer:** The **Database component**. It stores important information such as orders, stock levels and addresses that other parts of the system depend on.

# 4. Data and Information

## 4.1 Data

**Data** refers to raw, crude or unordered facts that have not yet been processed into meaningful information.

Data can exist in many forms, including:

> **Numbers, characters, symbols, images, audio and other observations**

Data is collected from different sources and then processed to obtain useful insights.

**Example:**

```
85, 92, 76, 88, 95
```

These numbers are data because, without context, we do not know what they represent.

---

## 4.2 Wrong Interpretation of Data

Data can be misunderstood if incorrect methods or techniques are used to process or interpret it.

For example, suppose a student's percentage increases by 10% compared with a previous examination. This may appear to indicate improvement. However, if the total marks in the second examination were considerably lower, the student's actual performance may have declined.

Therefore:

> **Incorrect interpretation of data → Incorrect information → Poor decision-making**

---

## 4.3 Role of Data

Data plays an important role in modern organizations because it:

- Supports decision-making.
- Helps identify problems.
- Supports presentations and reports.
- Helps generate theories and insights.
- Saves time by helping identify real problems.
- Helps organizations evaluate investments.
- Provides the basis for information and analysis.

---

## 4.4 Information

**Information is processed, organized and presented data placed in a specific context so that it becomes useful to the user.**

Information cannot exist without data because data is the raw material from which information is produced.

### Characteristics of Useful Information

**Timeliness:** Information should be available when required.

**Accuracy:** Information should be correct and properly organized.

**Completeness:** Information should contain all relevant information required for the particular purpose.

### Examples of Information

- Train schedules
- Geographical directions
- Payslips
- Bank passbooks
- Printed reports and documents

---

## 4.5 Role of Information

Information helps organizations and society by:

- Generating new ideas, theories and discoveries.
- Supporting research and allowing researchers to understand what has already been discovered.
- Supporting commerce and business.
- Improving organizational output.
- Creating new employment opportunities in areas such as data analysis, systems architecture, hardware engineering, software development and web design.

---

## 4.6 Data vs Information

|Data|Information|
|---|---|
|Raw facts|Processed data|
|May be unorganized|Organized and meaningful|
|May lack context|Presented in context|
|Used as input for processing|Used for understanding and decisions|
|Example: Individual sales entries|Example: Monthly sales report|

---

## 5. Case Study — Sales Numbers at SuperMart

SuperMart's billing counters generate thousands of individual entries containing item codes, quantities and prices.

The individual checkout entries are **data** because they are raw transaction facts.

At the end of the month, the system produces a report titled **"Top 10 Best-Selling Products in September."** This is **information** because the sales data has been processed and presented in a meaningful context.

If the report is generated using only the first two weeks of September, **completeness** is affected because the report does not represent the entire month.

# 6. Information Systems in Organizations

Information systems play an important role at every level of an organization.

They help organizations:

> **Perform operations → Store data → Communicate → Monitor performance → Analyze trends → Make decisions → Plan → Improve customer service → Reduce costs → Gain competitive advantage**

Companies such as **eBay, Amazon, Alibaba and Google** depend heavily on information systems for their operations.

# 7. Capabilities of Information Systems

### 7.1 Transaction Processing

Information systems can process large numbers of routine transactions quickly.

**Examples:** ATM transactions, online purchases, railway ticket bookings and salary processing.

### 7.2 Data Storage

Information systems can store large quantities of organizational data.

**Examples:** Customer databases, employee databases and student databases.

### 7.3 Data Retrieval

Users can quickly retrieve required information from stored data.

**Example:** Finding all customers who purchased a particular product during the previous month.

### 7.4 Data Processing

Systems can perform calculations and transform data.

**Examples:** Salary calculation, tax calculation, sales analysis and profit calculation.

### 7.5 Communication

Information systems facilitate communication through email, messaging, video conferencing and collaboration platforms.

### 7.6 Reporting

Systems can generate reports for users and managers.

**Examples:** Daily sales reports, monthly financial reports and employee attendance reports.

### 7.7 Decision Support

Information systems provide information and analytical tools that help managers make decisions.

**Example:** A manager compares sales across different regions before deciding where to increase marketing expenditure.

### 7.8 Forecasting

Historical data can be analyzed to predict future trends.

```
Historical Sales
       ↓
Data Analysis
       ↓
Forecast
       ↓
Expected Future Sales
```

**Example:** Previous sales data can be used to forecast future product demand.

# 8. Case Study — Core Banking at Sunrise Bank

Sunrise Bank's core banking system processes lakhs of ATM withdrawals and online transfers every day. It stores customer account histories, retrieves transaction records and automatically sends monthly statements. It also analyzes previous transaction patterns to forecast ATM cash requirements during festive seasons.

|Activity|IS Capability|
|---|---|
|Processing ATM withdrawals|**Transaction Processing**|
|Finding six months of transactions|**Data Retrieval**|
|Sending monthly statements|**Reporting**|
|Predicting festive-season cash demand|**Forecasting**|

**Key difference:** Reporting summarizes what has already happened, while forecasting uses historical data to estimate future trends.

## 9.1 Operational Level

The operational level deals with routine, day-to-day activities.

It is supported mainly by **Transaction Processing Systems (TPS)**.

**Examples:** Sales, payroll, order processing and inventory management.

**Users:** Operational employees and supervisors.

**Decisions:** Structured, repetitive and based on well-defined rules.

---

## 9.2 Knowledge Level

The knowledge level supports employees who create, integrate and share information.

It is supported by **Knowledge Work Systems (KWS)** and **Office Automation Systems (OAS)**.

**Examples:** CAD systems, document-management systems and other tools used by engineers, researchers and clerical staff.

---

## 9.3 Management/Tactical Level

The management level supports middle managers in monitoring and controlling operations.

It is supported by **Management Information Systems (MIS)** and **Decision Support Systems (DSS)**.

Typical outputs include:

- Summary reports
- Exception reports
- Analytical information
- Performance information

Decisions are generally **semi-structured**, such as budgeting and resource allocation.

---

## 9.4 Strategic Level

The strategic level supports senior executives and long-term organizational planning.

It is supported by **Executive Support Systems (ESS)** or **Executive Information Systems (EIS)**.

These systems provide high-level, summarized internal and external information.

**Examples of decisions:** Entering new markets, mergers, major investments and opening new facilities.

Decisions are generally **unstructured, non-routine and long-term**.

---

# 10. Data Requirements at Each Level

|Organizational Level|Main Data Requirement|Decision Type|
|---|---|---|
|**Operational**|Detailed, current transaction data|Structured|
|**Knowledge**|Specialized and technical information|Knowledge work|
|**Management**|Summarized, comparative and exception information|Semi-structured|
|**Strategic**|Highly summarized internal and external information|Unstructured|

### Easy way to remember

> **Lower level = detailed and frequent data** **Higher level = summarized and strategic information**

# 13. Information System Development

System development involves planning, designing, building, testing, implementing and maintaining information systems.

## 13.1 Systems Development Life Cycle — SDLC

```
Investigation
      ↓
Analysis
      ↓
Design
      ↓
Implementation
      ↓
Maintenance
```

**Investigation:** Identify the problem and determine whether a new system is required.

**Analysis:** Identify requirements and study existing processes.

**Design:** Design the system, database, interfaces and processes.

**Implementation:** Build, test, deploy and introduce the system to users.

**Maintenance:** Correct errors, improve the system and adapt it to changing requirements.

---

## 13.2 Development Approaches

### Waterfall

Uses a sequential process:

> Requirements → Design → Implementation → Testing → Deployment → Maintenance

### Agile

Uses iterative development with frequent feedback:

> Requirements → Design → Develop → Test → Deploy → Review → Repeat

### Prototyping

A preliminary version of the system is created, evaluated by users and refined before final implementation.

### End-User Development

Users themselves develop or customize applications to meet specific needs.

---

## 13.3 People Involved in Development

Systems analysts, programmers, project managers, database administrators and end users all contribute to system development.

The development process aims to ensure that the system meets business requirements, works properly and is delivered within the required time and budget.

### IT Governance

### Resource Management

Involves managing hardware, software, data, networks and human resources.

### Security Management

Protects information assets against threats and unauthorized access.

### Quality Management

Ensures that systems meet required standards of performance, reliability and quality.

### Global IS Management

# 16. Role and Process of Management

## 16.1 Who is a Manager?

A **manager** is responsible for planning, organizing, leading and controlling organizational resources to achieve objectives.

These resources include human, financial, physical and informational resources.

---

## 16.2 Management Process — POLC

### Planning

Setting objectives and deciding the best course of action to achieve them.

### Organizing

Arranging resources, responsibilities, tasks and authority so that plans can be implemented.

### Leading/Directing

Motivating, guiding and supervising employees toward organizational objectives.

### Controlling

Monitoring performance, comparing actual results with standards and taking corrective action.

```
Planning → Organizing → Leading → Controlling
                    ↑             ↓
                    └── Feedback ─┘
```

---

# 17. Managerial Roles — Mintzberg

Mintzberg classified managerial roles into **three categories containing ten roles**.

## 17.1 Interpersonal Roles

**Figurehead:** Performs symbolic and ceremonial duties.

**Leader:** Motivates, guides and directs employees.

**Liaison:** Maintains relationships and contacts outside the immediate chain of command.

---

## 17.2 Informational Roles

**Monitor:** Seeks and receives information.

**Disseminator:** Transmits information to employees and subordinates.

**Spokesperson:** Communicates information to outsiders.

---

## 17.3 Decisional Roles

**Entrepreneur:** Initiates change and improvement.

**Disturbance Handler:** Resolves conflicts and unexpected problems.

**Resource Allocator:** Decides how organizational resources should be distributed.

**Negotiator:** Represents the organization during negotiations.

---

# 19. Functions of a Manager

### Planning

Managers set goals, forecast future conditions, develop strategies and action plans, and budget resources.

### Organizing

Managers design organizational structures, delegate authority and responsibility, and group activities into departments.

### Staffing

Includes recruiting, selecting, training and developing employees, along with performance appraisal and compensation management.

### Directing/Leading

Managers supervise and guide employees, motivate staff, communicate effectively, build teams and resolve conflicts.

### Coordinating

Managers synchronize activities between departments and ensure that individual and organizational goals work together.

### Controlling

Managers establish standards, measure actual performance, compare results with standards and take corrective action when required.

# 20. Methods of Management

## 20.1 Classical Management Approaches

### Scientific Management — Frederick Taylor

Focuses on efficiency through **time-and-motion studies, standardization and task specialization**.

**Example:** A factory studies how workers perform a task and redesigns the process to reduce unnecessary movement and improve productivity.

### Administrative Management — Henri Fayol

Focuses on principles of management, including **division of work, authority, discipline, unity of command** and other principles.

### Bureaucratic Management — Max Weber

Emphasizes **formal rules, hierarchy, clear authority and division of labor**.

---

# 21. Behavioral/Human Relations Approaches

## Human Relations Movement — Elton Mayo

Emphasizes employee morale, social needs and group dynamics as factors affecting productivity. It is associated with the **Hawthorne Studies**.

## Theory X and Theory Y — Douglas McGregor

**Theory X** assumes employees may dislike work, avoid responsibility and require control.

**Theory Y** assumes employees can be self-motivated, accept responsibility and seek opportunities to contribute.

## Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Managers can understand employee motivation through different levels of needs:

```
Self-Actualization
       ↑
Esteem
       ↑
Social
       ↑
Safety
       ↑
Physiological
```

The levels range from basic physiological needs to safety, social belonging, esteem and finally self-actualization.

---

# 22. Quantitative/Management Science Approach

This approach uses **mathematical models, statistics and operations research** to support decision-making.

Examples include:

- Linear programming
- Queuing theory
- Simulation
- Statistical analysis

**Example:** A company can use mathematical models to determine the best allocation of limited resources.

# 23. Modern Management Methods

## Management by Objectives — MBO

Associated with **Peter Drucker**, MBO involves managers and employees jointly setting specific, measurable objectives and reviewing progress against them.

## Total Quality Management — TQM

TQM is an organization-wide commitment to **continuous improvement and customer satisfaction**, involving employees throughout the organization.

## Management by Walking Around — MBWA

Managers interact informally with employees at their workplaces to gain firsthand understanding of problems and operations.

## Agile/Lean Management

Focuses on flexibility, iterative work, rapid delivery, continuous feedback and reduction of unnecessary activities or waste. It is common in IT and software projects.

## Systems Approach

Views an organization as an **open system** whose different parts are interconnected and interact with the external environment.

```
Inputs → Organizational Processes → Outputs
   ↑                                  ↓
   └──────────── Feedback ────────────┘
```

A problem in one department can affect other departments because organizational parts are interdependent.

## Contingency/Situational Approach

There is **no single best way to manage**. The appropriate management method depends on the situation, environment, people and organizational requirements.

## Six Sigma

Six Sigma is a **data-driven method for reducing defects and improving process quality** through statistical analysis.

---

# 24. Case Study — Reducing Defects at Nova Auto Parts

Nova Auto Parts faced a high rate of defective components.

The company trained employees in Six Sigma and used statistical analysis to identify and reduce the causes of defects. At the same time, it adopted an organization-wide philosophy of continuous improvement and customer satisfaction.

**Q1. Which method uses statistical analysis to reduce defects?**

**Answer:** **Six Sigma.** It uses data and statistical techniques to reduce defects and improve quality.

**Q2. Which method emphasizes organization-wide continuous improvement and customer satisfaction?**

**Answer:** **Total Quality Management (TQM).**

**Q3. How do Six Sigma and TQM complement each other?**

**Answer:** TQM provides the organization-wide quality philosophy, while Six Sigma provides data-driven and statistical techniques for achieving measurable quality improvements.
