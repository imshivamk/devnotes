	## 1. Information System

- **Information System (IS):** A combination of hardware, software, data, people, networks and procedures that collects, processes, stores and distributes information. **Example:** Online banking, railway reservation.

## 2. Components of Information System

- **Hardware:** Physical devices used for input, processing, output and storage. **Example:** Computer, scanner, printer.
- **Software:** Programs and instructions that control hardware and process data. **Example:** Windows, inventory software.
- **System Software:** Software that manages computer hardware and basic operations. **Example:** Operating system.
- **Application Software:** Software designed to perform specific user/business tasks. **Example:** Inventory-management software.
- **Procedures:** Step-by-step instructions for using or operating an information system. **Example:** Steps for processing an order.
- **Database:** An organized collection of data that can be stored, accessed and managed efficiently. **Example:** Customer/order database.
- **Human Resources:** People who develop, operate, manage and use information systems. **Example:** Programmer, manager, accountant.
- **Telecommunications Network:** Technology that allows data and information to move between devices and users. **Example:** Internet, company network.

## 3. Data & Information

- **Data:** Raw, unprocessed facts without meaningful context. **Example:** `85, 92, 76, 88`.
- **Information:** Processed, organized data presented in a meaningful context for users. **Example:** Monthly sales report.
- **Timeliness:** Information should be available when it is needed. **Example:** Real-time stock availability.
- **Accuracy:** Information should be correct and reliable. **Example:** Correct salary calculation.
- **Completeness:** Information should contain all relevant data needed for its purpose. **Example:** Full-year sales report.
- **Data → Information:** Data is processed to produce useful information for understanding and decision-making. **Example:** Individual sales entries → Monthly sales report.

## 4. Information System Capabilities

- **Transaction Processing:** Quickly processes large numbers of routine transactions. **Example:** ATM withdrawal, online purchase.
- **Data Storage:** Stores large amounts of organizational data. **Example:** Employee database.
- **Data Retrieval:** Finds and accesses required stored information. **Example:** Finding a customer's previous orders.
- **Data Processing:** Performs calculations and transforms raw data into useful results. **Example:** Salary or tax calculation.
- **Communication:** Enables information sharing between people and systems. **Example:** Email, video conferencing.
- **Reporting:** Produces organized reports from stored data. **Example:** Daily sales report.
- **Decision Support:** Provides information and analysis to help managers make decisions. **Example:** Comparing regional sales before increasing marketing.
- **Forecasting:** Uses historical data to estimate future trends or events. **Example:** Predicting future product demand.

## 5. MIS Pyramid

- **Operational Level:** Handles routine day-to-day activities using detailed current data. **Example:** Sales, payroll, inventory.
- **TPS (Transaction Processing System):** Processes routine business transactions at the operational level. **Example:** Railway ticket booking.
- **Knowledge Level:** Supports employees who create, use and share specialized knowledge. **Example:** CAD system for engineers.
- **KWS (Knowledge Work System):** Supports professional knowledge workers in creating specialized information. **Example:** Engineering design system.
- **OAS (Office Automation System):** Supports office communication and document-related work. **Example:** Document-management system.
- **Management/Tactical Level:** Helps middle managers monitor, control and analyze organizational performance. **Example:** Budgeting and resource allocation.
- **MIS (Management Information System):** Provides summarized reports for routine management decisions. **Example:** Monthly sales report.
- **DSS (Decision Support System):** Provides analytical tools and information for semi-structured decisions. **Example:** Analyzing which region needs more resources.
- **Strategic Level:** Supports senior executives with long-term organizational planning. **Example:** Deciding whether to enter a new market.
- **ESS/EIS:** Provides highly summarized internal and external information to senior executives. **Example:** Executive dashboard showing market trends.
- **Structured Decision:** A routine decision based on clearly defined rules. **Example:** Reordering stock when inventory falls below a limit.
- **Semi-structured Decision:** A decision partly based on rules and partly on managerial judgment. **Example:** Allocating a department's budget.
- **Unstructured Decision:** A non-routine decision requiring significant judgment. **Example:** Opening a new manufacturing plant.
- **Easy Rule:** Lower levels use detailed and frequent data, while higher levels use summarized and strategic information.

## 6. Business Applications

- **Business Application:** An information system used to support a specific business function or process. **Example:** Accounting software.
- **Functional Application:** Supports one specific business function. **Example:** Human Resource Information System.
- **ERP:** Integrates multiple business functions into one system. **Example:** Connecting purchasing, inventory and finance.
- **CRM:** Manages customer interactions and relationships. **Example:** Tracking customer enquiries and purchases.
- **SCM:** Coordinates suppliers, production, inventory, logistics and distribution. **Example:** Tracking goods from supplier to customer.

## 7. System Development

- **System Development:** The process of planning, designing, building, testing, implementing and maintaining an information system. **Example:** Developing a new banking application.
- **SDLC:** A structured process for developing and maintaining information systems. **Example:** Investigation → Analysis → Design → Implementation → Maintenance.
- **Investigation:** Identifies the problem and determines whether a new system is needed. **Example:** Studying why an existing system is slow.
- **Analysis:** Determines system requirements and studies existing processes. **Example:** Interviewing employees about required features.
- **Design:** Specifies the system's database, interface, processes and architecture. **Example:** Designing an online shopping system.
- **Implementation:** Builds, tests, deploys and introduces the system to users. **Example:** Launching a new payroll system.
- **Maintenance:** Fixes errors and improves or adapts an existing system. **Example:** Adding a new payment method.
- **Waterfall:** A sequential development approach where each phase is completed before the next begins. **Example:** Requirements → Design → Development → Testing.
- **Agile:** An iterative approach that develops software in small cycles with frequent feedback. **Example:** Releasing new app features every few weeks.
- **Prototyping:** Creates an early model of a system to obtain feedback before final development. **Example:** Trial version of a mobile app.
- **End-User Development:** Allows users to create or customize applications for their own needs. **Example:** Employee-created Excel automation.

## 8. IS Management

- **IS Management:** Managing technology, information and people so that information systems support organizational objectives. **Example:** Managing company IT infrastructure.
- **IT Governance:** Ensures IT strategy and decisions are aligned with business goals. **Example:** Selecting technology that supports business expansion.
- **Resource Management:** Manages hardware, software, data, networks and human resources. **Example:** Managing company computers and servers.
- **Security Management:** Protects information and systems from threats and unauthorized access. **Example:** Passwords and access controls.
- **Quality Management:** Ensures systems meet required standards of performance and reliability. **Example:** Testing system performance.
- **Global IS Management:** Manages information systems across different countries and environments. **Example:** Managing IT systems of a multinational company.

# 9. Management & POLC

- **Manager:** A person who plans, organizes, leads and controls resources to achieve organizational objectives. **Example:** Branch manager.
- **Planning:** Setting objectives and deciding how to achieve them. **Example:** Preparing next year's sales targets.
- **Organizing:** Arranging resources, tasks, responsibilities and authority to implement plans. **Example:** Assigning employees to departments.
- **Leading/Directing:** Motivating, guiding and supervising employees toward goals. **Example:** Motivating a sales team.
- **Controlling:** Comparing actual performance with standards and taking corrective action. **Example:** Correcting a department that exceeds its budget.
- **POLC:** The basic management process of Planning, Organizing, Leading and Controlling. **Example:** Manager sets goals → assigns work → motivates staff → checks results.

## 10. Mintzberg's Managerial Roles

### Interpersonal

- **Figurehead:** Performs symbolic and ceremonial duties. **Example:** Attending an official company event.
- **Leader:** Motivates, guides and directs employees. **Example:** Training and motivating staff.
- **Liaison:** Maintains relationships with people outside the immediate work group. **Example:** Contacting a supplier.

### Informational

- **Monitor:** Searches for and receives useful information. **Example:** Reviewing sales reports.
- **Disseminator:** Passes important information to employees or subordinates. **Example:** Sharing new company policies with staff.
- **Spokesperson:** Communicates organizational information to outsiders. **Example:** Explaining company results to the media.

### Decisional

- **Entrepreneur:** Initiates improvements and changes. **Example:** Introducing a new service.
- **Disturbance Handler:** Resolves conflicts and unexpected problems. **Example:** Settling a dispute between employees.
- **Resource Allocator:** Decides how resources should be distributed. **Example:** Assigning training budgets.
- **Negotiator:** Represents the organization during negotiations. **Example:** Negotiating a supplier contract.

## 11. Managerial Functions

- **Staffing:** Recruiting, selecting, training and developing employees. **Example:** Hiring and training new workers.
- **Coordinating:** Synchronizing activities between departments to achieve common goals. **Example:** Coordinating sales and production.
- **Directing:** Guiding and supervising employees to accomplish objectives. **Example:** Giving instructions to employees.
- **Controlling:** Measuring performance against standards and correcting deviations. **Example:** Comparing actual sales with targets.

# 12. Management Approaches

- **Scientific Management — Taylor:** Improves efficiency through task specialization, standardization and time-and-motion studies. **Example:** Redesigning factory work to reduce unnecessary movement.
- **Administrative Management — Fayol:** Focuses on general principles such as division of work, authority and unity of command. **Example:** Clearly assigning authority to departments.
- **Bureaucratic Management — Weber:** Uses formal rules, hierarchy, authority and division of labor. **Example:** Government organization with clearly defined positions.
- **Human Relations — Mayo:** Emphasizes employee morale, social needs and group relationships in productivity. **Example:** Improving teamwork and employee relationships.
- **Theory X — McGregor:** Assumes employees may dislike work and require close supervision. **Example:** Strict supervision of workers.
- **Theory Y — McGregor:** Assumes employees can be self-motivated and accept responsibility. **Example:** Giving employees autonomy.
- **Maslow's Hierarchy:** Explains motivation through physiological, safety, social, esteem and self-actualization needs. **Example:** Salary → job security → teamwork → recognition → personal growth.
- **Quantitative Approach:** Uses mathematics, statistics and models to support management decisions. **Example:** Linear programming for resource allocation.
- **MBO:** Managers and employees jointly set measurable objectives and review performance against them. **Example:** Setting a target to increase sales by 10%.
- **TQM:** An organization-wide approach focused on continuous improvement and customer satisfaction. **Example:** All departments working to reduce defects.
- **MBWA:** Managers walk around and interact directly with employees to understand operations. **Example:** Manager visiting the factory floor.
- **Agile/Lean Management:** Uses flexibility, iteration, rapid feedback and waste reduction to improve work. **Example:** Software team delivering features in short cycles.
- **Systems Approach:** Views an organization as interconnected parts that interact with the external environment. **Example:** A production problem affecting sales and inventory.
- **Contingency Approach:** States that the best management method depends on the situation and environment. **Example:** Using strict control in an emergency but participative management normally.
- **Six Sigma:** A data-driven method that uses statistical analysis to reduce defects and improve quality. **Example:** An automobile company analyzing defect causes to reduce faulty parts.
