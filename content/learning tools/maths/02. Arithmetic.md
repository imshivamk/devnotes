### 1. Ratios and Proportions

Ratios compare two quantities by division, while proportions indicate the equality of two ratios.

#### **A. Core Concepts & Formulas**

- **Ratio**: The ratio of $a$ to $b$ is written as $a : b = \frac{a}{b}$.
    
- **Proportion**: If $a : b = c : d$, then $ad = bc$ (Product of extremes = Product of means).
    
- **Duplicate Ratio**: The duplicate ratio of $a : b$ is $a^{2} : b^{2}$.
    
- **Compounded Ratio**: The compounded ratio of $(a : b)$ and $(c : d)$ is $ac : bd$.
    
- **Variations**:
    
    - **Direct Variation**: $x \propto y \Rightarrow x = ky$.
        
    - **Inverse Variation**: $x \propto \frac{1}{y} \Rightarrow x = \frac{k}{y}$.
        

#### **B. Special Cases**

- **Mean Proportional**: The mean proportional between $a$ and $b$ is $\sqrt{ab}$.
    
- **Third Proportional**: If $a : b = b : c$, then $c$ is the third proportional, where $c = \frac{b^{2}}{a}$.
    

> **Solved Example**: If $A : B = 2 : 3$ and $B : C = 4 : 5$, find $A : B : C$.
> 
> **Step 1**: To combine ratios, make the common term ($B$) equal.
> 
> **Step 2**: Multiply $A : B$ by 4 and $B : C$ by 3.
> 
> **Step 3**: $A : B = 8 : 12$; $B : C = 12 : 15$.
> 
> **Solution**: $A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15$.

### 2. Percentage

Percentage is a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100.

#### **A. Core Concepts & Formulas**

- **Percentage Change**: $\frac{\text{Change}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100$.
    
- **Successive Percentage Change**: If a value is increased by $x\%$ and then $y\%$, the net change is $(x + y + \frac{xy}{100})\%$.
    
- **Converting to Fractions**: $x\% = \frac{x}{100}$. (Example: $25\% = \frac{1}{4}$).
    

#### **B. Special Cases**

- **Product Constancy**: If the price of a commodity increases by $R\%$, the reduction in consumption needed to keep expenditure the same is $(\frac{R}{100+R} \times 100)\%$.
    
- **Population Formula**: If population $P$ increases at $r\%$ per annum:
    
    - Population after $n$ years: $P(1 + \frac{r}{100})^{n}$.
        
    - Population $n$ years ago: $\frac{P}{(1 + \frac{r}{100})^{n}}$.
        

> **Solved Example**: A man spends 35% of his income on food and 25% on rent. If he saves ₹4,000, find his total income.
> 
> **Step 1**: Total expenditure = $35\% + 25\% = 60\%$.
> 
> **Step 2**: Total savings = $100\% - 60\% = 40\%$.
> 
> **Step 3**: $40\%$ of Income = ₹4,000 $\Rightarrow$ Income $= \frac{4000 \times 100}{40}$.
> 
> **Solution**: Total Income = ₹10,000.

### 3. Problems on Time and Work

This topic relates the amount of work done to the time taken and the efficiency of the worker.

#### **A. Core Concepts & Formulas**

- **Work Rate**: If a person can do a piece of work in $n$ days, work done in 1 day $= \frac{1}{n}$.
    
- **Combined Work**: If $A$ takes $x$ days and $B$ takes $y$ days, together they take $\frac{xy}{x+y}$ days.
    
- **Efficiency**: If $A$ is $k$ times as good a workman as $B$, then $A$ will take $\frac{1}{k}$ of the time taken by $B$.
    

#### **B. Special Cases (Chain Rule)**

- **Work Formula**: $\frac{M_{1}D_{1}H_{1}}{W_{1}} = \frac{M_{2}D_{2}H_{2}}{W_{2}}$, where $M = \text{Men}$, $D = \text{Days}$, $H = \text{Hours}$, and $W = \text{Work}$.
    

> **Solved Example**: $A$ can finish a work in 10 days and $B$ in 15 days. How long will they take working together?
> 
> **Step 1**: $A$'s 1-day work = $1/10$; $B$'s 1-day work = $1/15$.
> 
> **Step 2**: Together 1-day work $= \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{15} = \frac{3+2}{30} = \frac{5}{30} = \frac{1}{6}$.
> 
> **Solution**: They will take 6 days together.

### 4. Problems on Time, Distance, and Speed

These problems involve moving objects and the relationship between speed, time, and distance.

#### **A. Core Concepts & Formulas**

- **Basic Formula**: $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$.
    
- **Unit Conversion**:
    
    - $1 \text{ km/hr} = \frac{5}{18} \text{ m/s}$.
        
    - $1 \text{ m/s} = \frac{18}{5} \text{ km/hr}$.
        
- **Average Speed**: $\frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}$.
    
    - If a body covers two equal distances at speeds $u$ and $v$, Average Speed $= \frac{2uv}{u+v}$.
        

#### **B. Special Cases (Relative Speed & Trains)**

- **Relative Speed**:
    
    - Same direction: $u - v$.
        
    - Opposite direction: $u + v$.
        
- **Train crossing a platform**: Total Distance $= \text{Length of Train} + \text{Length of Platform}$.
    

> **Solved Example**: A train 150m long passes a pole in 9 seconds. Find the speed of the train in km/hr.
> 
> **Step 1**: $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{150}{9} \text{ m/s}$.
> 
> **Step 2**: Convert to km/hr: $\frac{150}{9} \times \frac{18}{5} = \frac{30}{1} \times 2 = 60$.
> 
> **Solution**: Speed = 60 km/hr.
