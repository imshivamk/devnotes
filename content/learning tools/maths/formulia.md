|Discriminant of Quadratic equation $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$|The Discriminant of the quadratic equation is given by $D=b^{2}-4ac$|
|Nature of Roots|$\bullet$ $D=0$, the roots are real and equal $\alpha=\beta=-\frac{b}{2a}$|
||If $D\ne0$, The roots are real and unequal.|
||$\bullet$ If $D>0$ and $D$ is a perfect square, the roots are rational and unequal.|
||$\bullet$ If $D>0$ and $D$ is not a perfect square, the roots are irrational and unequal.|
||$\bullet$ If $D<0$, the roots are imaginary and unequal.|
|Formation of Quadratic Equation with given roots|$(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)=0$; $x^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)x+\alpha\beta=0$|
||$x^{2}$ (Sum of roots)$x+$ product of roots$=0$|
|Common Roots|$\bullet$ If two quadratic equations $a_{1}x^{2}+b_{1}x+c_{1}=0$ and $a_{2}x^{2}+b_{2}x+c_{2}=0$ have both roots common, then $\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}=\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}$.|
||$\bullet$ If only one root $\alpha$ is common, then $\alpha=\frac{c_{1}a_{2}-c_{2}a_{1}}{a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}}=\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{c_{1}a_{2}-c_{2}a_{1}}$|

||$\bullet$ If $-\frac{b}{2a}\in[x_{1},x_{2}]$ then, $f(x)\in[\min{f(x_{1}), f(x_{2}),-\frac{D}{4a} }, \max{f(x_{1}), f(x_{2}), -\frac{D}{4a}}]$|
|Roots under special cases for $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$|$\bullet$ If $c=0$ then one root is zero. Other root is $-\frac{b}{a}$|
||$\bullet$ If $b= 0$ The roots are equal but in opposite signs.|
||$\bullet$ If $b=c=0$, then both roots are zero.|
||$\bullet$ If $a=c$, then the roots are reciprocal to each other.|
||$\bullet$ If $a+b+c=0$, then one root is $1$ and the second root is $\frac{c}{a}$.|
||$\bullet$ If $a=b=c=0$ then the equation will become an identity and will satisfy every value of $x$.|
|Graph of Quadratic equation $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$|The graph of a quadratic equation $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$ is a parabola.|
||$\bullet$ If $a>0$ then the graph of a quadratic equation will be concave upwards.|
||$\bullet$ If $a<0$ then the graph of a quadratic equation will be concave downwards.|
|Maximum and Minimum value for $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$|$\bullet$ If $a<0$ then the expression has the greatest value at $x=-\frac{b}{2a}$. The maximum value is $-\frac{D}{4a}$.|
||$\bullet$ If $a>0$, then the expression has the least value at $x=-\frac{b}{2a}$. The minimum value is $-\frac{D}{4a}$.|
|Quadratic Expression in Two Variables|The general form of a quadratic equation in two variables $x$ and $y$ is $ax^{2}+2hxy+by^{2}+2gx+2fy+c$.|
|Condition to resolve the expression into two linear rational factors|$\Delta=0$ where $\Delta=\begin{vmatrix}a&h&g\h&b&f\g&f&c\end{vmatrix}=0$ which is $abc+2fgh-af^{2}-bg^{2}-ch^{2}=0$ and $h^{2}-ab>0$. This is called the Discriminant of the given expression.|
